Career Scout: Read a Company in Three Minutes

Judging one company means visiting five or more platforms, where information is fragmented and contradictory. Career Scout splits the task into five dimensions, assigns an agent to each in parallel, then lets a controller agent resolve the conflicts.

RoleProduct design + agent orchestration
ContextHackathon
StackMulti-Agent · Prompt orchestration
FormWeb retrieval product

01The problem isn't search — it's judgment

What actually blocks a job seeker doing background research is not missing information, but too much of it in conflict: the hiring platform promises generous pay, the employee forum reports heavy overtime, and the registry shows a few lawsuits you can't tell whether to worry about.

So the core to solve was never "search faster" but "judge which source to believe for the user". That positioning drove every design decision after it.

02Splitting "trustworthy" into five dimensions

A vague "is it trustworthy" cannot be searched directly; it has to be cut into independently verifiable dimensions. We settled on five:

  1. Registry risk: registration details, equity changes, litigation and operating anomalies
  2. Sentiment: negative signals from public reporting and social platforms
  3. Pay: salary ranges compared across platforms
  4. Interview process: length of the loop and candidate experience feedback
  5. Employee reviews: sentiment from current and former employees

Splitting dimensions gives each agent a clear task boundary, lets prompts be written specifically, and lets output formats be constrained uniformly — which makes conflict resolution downstream far easier.

03Agent orchestration and conflict resolution

Five sub-agents search in parallel while the controller agent handles three things: dispatching tasks, converging output format, and handling conflicts between dimensions.

Conflict resolution is the most valuable part of this product. When the pay dimension shows industry-leading numbers while employee reviews turn heavily negative, the controller doesn't average them — it preserves the disagreement, annotates source and date, and hands the basis for judgment back to the user.

The value of multi-agent isn't faster retrieval — it's having something judge which source to believe.

05Retrospective

My biggest takeaway: the cost of a multi-agent architecture goes into making outputs comparable, not into making the agents run. The cleaner the dimension split, the lighter the conflict resolution.

Engineering isn't my destination, but it sets how far I can think — without knowing what agent orchestration can do, I would have positioned this as an aggregated search instead of a product that judges on the user's behalf.

This is a hackathon build, not yet a sustainable product. Happy to talk through the prompt design or how we weighted the dimensions.
