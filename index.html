02 / Trajectory

Journey

My Trajectory

From a freshman who could only write code, to someone who can turn "users can't find parking fees" into a city-by-city strategy — here is how that line was drawn.

Schematic · not to scale

  1. 2023.09

    Entered CUHK-Shenzhen, Computer Science & Engineering

    I arrived thinking product work meant drawing wireframes. My first group project changed that: every feature shipped, and nobody wanted to use it. After that I forced myself to ask "whose problem is this" before writing a line of code.

  2. 2025

    China Undergraduate Mathematical Contest in Modeling · First Prize, Guangdong

    The hardest part of those three days was not the modeling — it was deciding which variables to drop. We started by stuffing in every factor and only got a stable result after cutting half our assumptions. That was the first time I felt that trade-offs beat completeness.

  3. 2024–2026 academic year

    Dean's List

    That year I spent most of my hours on data analysis and algorithms, and it settled the question: I want the product role closest to the data, not a purely business-logic coding job.

  4. 2026.05 – 2026.08

    Baidu IDG Maps · Strategy Product Manager Intern

    My first assignment was concrete: why can't users find parking fees on the map? Over three months I benchmarked 500 lots against competitors, designed a multi-source collection strategy, and held the quality line on a 130-person collection project — pushing coverage from 27% to 45%. This is where I learned that strategy has to land on people and cost.

  5. 2026

    MCM Meritorious Winner & Hackathon project "Career Scout"

    Modeling contests made me braver with uncertainty; the hackathon let me actually build something — a multi-agent product that helps job seekers read a company clearly. Engineering isn't my destination, but it sets how far I can think.

Three things this line taught me

  • Tier the problem before touching it

    Three-dimension tiering across 150 cities and a five-source framework were both about slicing an oversized goal into prioritizable pieces.

  • Trust data, but chase the root cause

    57% of 23 anomalous records had nothing to do with opening hours — the real defect sat in source-link recall. Numbers are only the entrance.

  • Strategy has to land on people and cost

    Running a 130-person collection project from PRD to quality acceptance taught me to price the labour before arguing about elegance.

03 / Deep Dive

Deep Dive

Three Months on Baidu Maps

The first question I was handed: why can't users find parking fees on the map? Below is how that single complaint became a strategy that shipped across 150 cities.

Baidu · IDG Maps Business Group

Strategy Product Manager Intern

2026.05 – 2026.08

Coverage 27% → 45%, ahead of competitors by 10+ PP

Problem / Context

Parking fee coverage on Baidu Maps sat around 27%, and "no fee information" was a top complaint in the navigate-to-destination scenario.

Strategy Design

  1. Sampled 500 parking lots against Amap and Tencent Maps to locate where the coverage gap concentrated
  2. Scenario-specific collection: competitor mining, field survey, roadside official sites, and AI phone inquiry
  3. Three-dimension tiering across 150 cities, launched in priority waves to control labour cost and risk

Going Further

Built a quality feedback loop for AI phone inquiry, identified five recurring failure classes, and drew the human–machine boundary that supports an 80%+ automation rate.

Coverage lifted at zero incremental collection cost

Problem / Context

Manual production volume was off by an order of magnitude. Tracing 23 anomalous records showed 57% were unrelated to opening hours — the root cause was a defect in source-link recall.

Strategy Design

  1. Five-source framework: barrier-breaking crawl, parent-child inheritance, brand sweeping, obvious-fake filtering, competitor mining
  2. Core principle "zero incremental collection cost, maximum coverage" — reuse existing data relations first
  3. Validated with ground-truth sampling before opening the full pipeline
